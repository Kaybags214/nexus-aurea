# Check 1 — Parties, identifiers and declarations

- [ ] Seller / shipper name and full address
- [ ] Buyer / consignee name and full address
- [ ] Ship-to party where it differs from the buyer
- [ ] Invoice number
- [ ] Invoice date
- [ ] Purchase order or contract reference where the trade uses one

## Identifiers

- [ ] Tax or trade identifiers as the destination requires — EORI, VAT, IRS/EIN, importer of record
- [ ] Importer of record clearly identified where it is not the consignee

A shipment whose importer of record cannot be determined from the paperwork will be held. Treat
an absent one as **Major**, rising to Critical where the destination requires it on the invoice.

## Declarations

- [ ] Statement of origin present where required
- [ ] Any preference or free-trade-agreement claim stated, with the agreement named
- [ ] Signature or authorised statement where the destination requires one

## Reason for export

- [ ] Reason for export stated — sale, sample, return, repair, clinical trial material

For pharma this matters more than usual: samples, returns and clinical supply are valued and
treated differently from a commercial sale. An invoice for clinical trial material described as
a plain sale is a **Major** finding.
