# Check 1 — Is a Shipper's Declaration required?

Decide this before auditing anything else. It determines whether a missing DGD is a Critical
finding or correct behaviour.

## The two cases

**Dry ice as a refrigerant for non-dangerous contents.**
No Shipper's Declaration is required. The AWB entry carries the dry ice. A missing DGD here is
**not** a finding.

**Contents are themselves dangerous goods.**
The DGD is required, and dry ice is declared on it alongside the other entries.

## The pharma trap

**UN3373 Biological Substance Category B shipped on dry ice needs no DGD at all.**
PI 650 does not require a declaration, and dry ice as a pure refrigerant does not either.
Both halves are non-declarable, so the correct outcome is no DGD — but the AWB must still carry
the UN1845 entry with net kg per package.

A reviewer who half-remembers "UN3373 is dangerous goods" will raise a false Critical here.
Do not be that reviewer. Check what the contents actually are.

## Record it

State which case you concluded and the evidence you concluded it from. Carry that decision into
checks 4 and 5 — it changes which documents must show a dry ice weight.
