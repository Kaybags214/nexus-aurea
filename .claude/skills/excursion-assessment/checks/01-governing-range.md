# Check 1 — Establish the governing range

Before any number means anything, fix what the product was supposed to stay within.

- [ ] Product's labelled storage condition identified, and the source named
- [ ] Range stated with both bounds and units (e.g. **+2 °C to +8 °C**)
- [ ] Any permitted excursion allowance on the label recorded separately from the range itself
- [ ] Shipping condition identified where it differs from storage (frozen, controlled ambient)

## Precedence

Per `standards-of-precedence.md` section 2, in order:

1. Product label / marketing authorisation storage condition — **the floor, never widened**
2. Destination-country GDP/GMP regulation
3. IATA CEIV Pharma / Temperature Control Regulations for the air leg
4. WHO TRS 961 Annex 9 / USP <1079>
5. Customer SOP or quality agreement, where stricter

**The narrowest range wins.** A shipment meeting the carrier's SOP but breaching the label
fails.

## When the label range cannot be established

Stop and say so. An excursion cannot be assessed against an unknown range, and assuming
+2/+8 because the product "looked like a fridge item" is exactly the guess
`skill-contract.md` rule 2 forbids. Ask for the label or the MA.
