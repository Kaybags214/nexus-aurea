# Check 5 — Documentation

## Is a Shipper's Declaration required?

**Section II shipments do not normally require a Shipper's Declaration.** Section IA and IB do.

- [ ] Section determined in check 1
- [ ] Presence or absence of a DGD is consistent with that section

Do **not** raise a missing-DGD finding on a Section II shipment. This is the same trap as dry
ice as a refrigerant, and the same discipline applies: establish the case first, then judge the
document against it.

Conversely, a DGD supplied for a Section II shipment is an over-declaration — worth noting, not
a safety defect.

## Air Waybill

- [ ] AWB carries the lithium battery entry where required for the section
- [ ] UN number(s) shown
- [ ] Aircraft limitation shown where one applies (check 3)
- [ ] Number of packages shown

## Where a DGD is present (Section IA / IB)

Hand the declaration itself to `dgd-check` — classification, aircraft limitation, emergency
contact, signature and form condition are its job. Note here only whether the lithium-specific
content is right.

## Document of compliance and test summary

- [ ] Note whether a **lithium battery test summary** is available on request

The test summary is a manufacturer obligation rather than a document that travels with every
shipment. Its absence from the submitted set is not automatically a finding — but where the
shipper cannot produce one on request, that is worth recording as a question to the shipper.
