# Dry Ice (UN1845) — Documentation and Labeling Reference

Purpose: Regulatory requirements summary for shipping dry ice (carbon dioxide, solid) as a coolant, for use alongside `audit-lab/dry-ice-un1845/` practice files.

## When a Shipper's Declaration Is Required

- Required when dry ice is used to cool goods that are themselves dangerous goods.
- Not required when dry ice is used solely to refrigerate non-dangerous goods — but package marking and shipping paper notation are still required.

## Mandatory Package Markings

- Proper shipping name: "Dry Ice" or "Carbon Dioxide, Solid" — must appear on the same surface as the hazard label
- UN number: "UN1845"
- Net weight of dry ice in kilograms
- Name and address of shipper and consignee

## Class 9 Hazard Label

- Minimum dimension: 100mm x 100mm (measured corner to corner along one side)
- Do not write inside the diamond border of the label
- Label must be affixed to the same surface as the proper shipping name marking

## Packaging Requirements

- Packaging must permit release of CO2 gas (vented) to prevent pressure buildup that could rupture the package
- Maximum net dry ice per package: 200 kg (operator or state variations may impose a lower cap — verify before shipping)

## Documentation to Accompany Shipment

- Shipping papers/manifest referencing UN1845 and net weight
- Shipper's Declaration for Dangerous Goods, if the coolant is accompanying dangerous goods (see `dgr/03-shipper-declarations/`)
- Safety data sheet, if requested by carrier or destination

## Review / Practice Checklist

- Does the marked net weight match the actual weighed quantity?
- Is the package vented (not sealed airtight)?
- Does the Class 9 label meet the minimum size requirement and avoid text inside the diamond?
- If the dry ice is cooling a dangerous good, is there a matching DGD referencing both UN numbers?
- Is the total dry ice quantity within the 200 kg cap (and any lower operator/state limit)?
