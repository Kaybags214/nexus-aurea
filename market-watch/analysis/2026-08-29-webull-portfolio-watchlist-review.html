The nuclear pair, corrected

Your watchlist flags OKLO and SMR for "cash burn" and "dilution." The filings say the risk is somewhere else.

OKLO — solvency is not the risk

FY2026 P2 · $40.14 · −79.3% from high

Net assets per share of $17.69 across 186.0M shares imply roughly $3.3B of net assets, against operating cash flow of −$0.257/share (~$47.8M for the period). Debt-to-assets is 2.5% — effectively unlevered.

The real risk is the price you pay for it: P/B 2.27 and a price-to-sales ratio of 6,175×, because revenue is a rounding error. Burn is accelerating (−$0.103/sh the prior period), but this company is not running out of money soon. Re-frame the watch rule from cash runway to valuation and permitting milestones.

SMR — revenue is going backwards

FY2026 P2 · $9.29 · −83.8% from high

Same balance-sheet picture: net assets per share $4.80 over 429.7M shares (~$2.1B), debt-to-assets 1.7%, operating cash flow −$0.135/share. Funded.

But the Q3 estimate, due 4–9 Nov, is $3.81M revenue against $8.24M a year ago — a 54% decline. For a pre-commercial infrastructure name whose entire thesis is proof of deployment, revenue halving is a more serious signal than the 84% drawdown. This is the single most important date on your calendar.

What the data does not say

Four limits worth carrying forward.
  • One session is not a trend. Every flow reading here covers 26–28 August. Intel's +$807M reversing in a day is the demonstration of why a single print should not move a thesis.
  • Order-size buckets are inference, not identity. "Large order" is a proxy for institutional activity. It is not 13F data, and it does not tell you who traded or why.
  • Net assets are not cash. The OKLO and SMR balance-sheet figures are derived from per-share indicators and share counts; runway needs the actual cash line from the 10-Q before you rely on it.
  • Analyst targets lag. Several here were stamped before Friday's move. Coherent's was set 27 August, one day before the stock fell through its lowest target.
