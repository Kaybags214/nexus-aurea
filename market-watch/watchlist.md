## Filing Types to Track

- 8-K: Major corporate events
- 10-Q: Quarterly report
- 10-K: Annual report
- DEF 14A: Proxy statement, governance, executive compensation
- Form 4: Insider transactions
- S-1: Registration statement / IPO filing
- S-3: Shelf registration
- 424B: Prospectus supplement / offering detail

## Review Rules

- Do not rely on price alone.
- Read filings before forming opinions.
- Track dilution, debt, cash flow, management changes, risk factors, and business model changes.
- Separate speculation from evidence.
- Watch ownership changes, share issuance, capital raises, and regulatory issues.
- Treat hype sectors carefully.
- Treat microcap / low-price stocks as high-risk until filings prove stability.
- Treat newly public infrastructure names as watch-first until valuation, filings, and lockup / insider activity are understood.
- Treat biotech names as milestone-driven: watch clinical data, regulatory events, safety signals, cash runway, dilution, and commercialization progress before investing.
- Treat cold-chain logistics names as operating-infrastructure watches: focus on revenue quality, margin trend, cash flow, customer demand, trial activity, and strategic partnerships.
- Treat employer-related stocks carefully: separate personal workplace experience from public filing evidence, and never trade on non-public workplace information.
- Treat mature enterprise technology names as cash-flow and execution watches: focus on durable software growth, free cash flow, debt, dividend coverage, and whether AI claims turn into measurable revenue.
- Treat semiconductor turnaround names as prove-it watches: focus on process-node execution, foundry customer wins, gross margin recovery, free cash flow, capex discipline, and competitive position.
- Treat industrial photonics names as cycle-sensitive infrastructure watches: focus on order demand, gross margin, China exposure, automation demand, cash flow, and whether growth products become material.
- Treat ETFs as benchmark baskets, not operating companies: focus on holdings, concentration, overlap with other funds, expense ratio, sector drawdown risk, and whether the ETF improves diversification.
- Treat defense technology names as contract-and-execution watches: focus on backlog, book-to-bill, production scaling, contract timing, budget exposure, margins, cash flow, R&D intensity, dilution, and valuation.
- Treat major pharma platform names as regulated-medicine infrastructure watches: focus on commercialization, supply capacity, reimbursement, clinical pipeline durability, regulatory updates, litigation, margins, and competitive pressure.
- Treat advanced nuclear names as pre-commercial infrastructure watches: focus on permitting, DOE/NRC authorization, fuel supply, construction execution, customer agreements, cash runway, dilution, safety milestones, and proof of first power.
- Treat public quantum names as high-risk milestone watches: focus on cash runway, dilution, customer validation, government contracts, system performance, technical roadmaps, and whether revenue is real or mostly hype.
- Treat private-company signals as research context only: they are not public-stock entries unless a public security exists; use them to understand sector direction, founder movement, funding, partnerships, and technology claims.
- Treat held positions as operating theses rather than benchmark baskets: anything actually owned needs a written risk case, a sizing rationale, and an exit condition, even when it is an ETF.
- Treat synthetic or swap-based funds as counterparty exposures: establish what share of exposure is derivative rather than physical, who the counterparties are, when the swaps mature, and whether the fund is able to hold the underlying securities directly at all.
- Treat memory semiconductor exposure as a pricing-cycle watch: focus on DRAM/NAND contract pricing, HBM ramp and qualification, supplier capex discipline, inventory correction risk, hyperscaler demand, and single-country concentration in Korea, Taiwan and China.
- Treat ADRs and foreign listings as translation watches: confirm the share ratio and float against the home listing, check whether quoted market value reflects the ADR or the whole company, watch premium or discount to the home line, and convert analyst targets and financials out of the home currency before comparing them to the US price.
