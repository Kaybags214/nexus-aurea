# 05 — Eligibility & Registrations

**Start this in week one.** It is the least interesting document in this folder and the one most
likely to cost you a submission window. Registration lead times are measured in weeks, are outside
your control, and no amount of good writing shortens them.

**All specifics below need verification against NSF 26-510 and the registration sites themselves.**
`nsf.gov` and `seedfund.nsf.gov` are blocked by this environment's egress proxy, so nothing here
comes from NSF directly.

---

## 1. Eligibility — confirm before writing anything

Standard SBIR criteria, to verify against the solicitation:

| Requirement | Typical rule | Nexus Aurea |
|---|---|---|
| Organisation type | US small business concern, organised for profit, US-based operations | Inc. — confirm |
| Ownership | More than 50% owned and controlled by US citizens or permanent residents | Confirm |
| Size | Fewer than 500 employees including affiliates | Yes |
| **PI primary employment** | **PI must be primarily employed by the company at the time of award and for the award's duration** — commonly ≥ 50% | **Confirm. See below** |
| Work location | R&D performed in the US | Yes |
| Phase I subaward cap | A ceiling applies on work subcontracted out | Relevant if a calibration collaborator is a subawardee |

**The PI employment condition is the one to check early.** If the PI holds other full-time
employment at the time of award, primary-employment cannot be met, and that constrains *when* an
award can be accepted — not whether a pitch can be submitted. Resolve it before the full proposal,
not after a recommendation. It also interacts with `01`'s team gap: if a calibration collaborator
joins as a subawardee rather than an employee, the subaward cap applies.

## 2. Registrations — the actual schedule risk

Sequential. Each depends on the previous. Reported lead times vary and have been long:

| # | Registration | Depends on | Typical lead time |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **UEI** (Unique Entity ID, via SAM.gov) | Legal entity details, address | Days |
| 2 | **SAM.gov entity registration** | UEI; EIN; **bank details for electronic funds transfer**; entity validation | **Weeks. Frequently the long pole** |
| 3 | **SBA Company Registry** (SBIR.gov) | UEI | Days — produces a control ID the proposal requires |
| 4 | **Research.gov / NSF account** | Entity registered | Days |
| 5 | **Login.gov / MFA** for the above | — | Same day |

**SAM.gov is where schedules die.** Entity validation can require documentary evidence that the
legal name and address match public records exactly. A mismatch between the name on the incorporation
paperwork and the name on a utility bill can cost weeks. SAM registration also **expires annually** —
if the entity was registered before and lapsed, renewal is not instant.

**Do this in week one regardless of which deadline you target.** It is free, it is independent of
every other decision in this folder, and it converts a hard schedule risk into a solved problem.

## 3. Deadline arithmetic

Reported full-proposal deadlines: **2026-11-04**, **2027-03-04**, then first Wednesday in July, first
Wednesday in November, first Thursday in March. Today is **2026-09-06**.

| Milestone | For 2026-11-04 | For 2027-03-04 |
|---|---|---|
| Registrations complete | **immediately** | by early October |
| Project Pitch submitted | within days | by mid-December |
| Invitation received | early October | mid-January |
| Full proposal written | ~4 weeks | ~6 weeks |
| Prototype + interviews informing it | **no** | **yes** |

**Target 2027-03-04.** The November window requires submitting a pitch before the risk framing is
sharp, before the prototype exists, and before anyone has been interviewed — spending one of two
annual pitch allocations on the weakest version of the idea.

The March window is not a delay. It is the difference between a proposal that asserts and one that
shows numbers.

## 4. Also confirm

- **Project Pitch caps** — reported as two per company per 12 months, three per technology. Confirm,
  because it governs how carefully to treat each submission.
- **Invitation validity** — reported as valid for two submission dates. Confirm; it affects whether
  a November invitation can carry to March.
- **Phase I ceiling and duration** — reported as up to $305,000 over 6–18 months.
- **Unallowable costs** — `00-track-separation.md` §4.
- **Topic area fit.** NSF SBIR is organised by topic. Identify which one this falls under before
  writing; the pitch is routed to a program director by topic, and the wrong topic gets the wrong
  reader. Worth one email to a program director in advance — NSF program staff generally welcome a
  short fit question, and it is free.

## 5. Week-one checklist

- [ ] Confirm entity details: exact legal name, EIN, address as filed
- [ ] Obtain UEI
- [ ] Begin SAM.gov registration — **do not defer this**
- [ ] Confirm ownership percentages meet the citizenship requirement
- [ ] Assess PI primary-employment position honestly, at award time
- [ ] Register in the SBA Company Registry once UEI issues
- [ ] Create Research.gov and Login.gov accounts
- [ ] Read NSF 26-510 in full, directly — not summaries, this one included
- [ ] Identify the topic area and the program director
- [ ] Start the corpus (`01` §6) and the interviews (`04`) in parallel — they do not wait on any of
      the above
